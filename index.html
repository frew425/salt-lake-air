How the numbers are worked out

Your station. Current readings come from the Utah DAQ air monitoring station you choose. Model forecasts are fetched for five areas of the Salt Lake and Utah valleys, and nearby monitors share one, because the models are much coarser than the distance between neighbouring stations. The official forecast follows the station's county, and the Clearing Index is shared across airshed 8. Your choice is stored only in this browser. The link in the address bar carries the monitor and chart, so it can be shared.

AQI. The big number is the US AQI for fine particles (PM2.5), using EPA's current breakpoints, where 0 to 9 µg/m³ is Good. It uses EPA's NowCast: a weighted average of the last 12 hours that leans on the most recent ones when levels are changing fast.

Hourly forecast. The next 24 hours use NOAA's hourly PM2.5 wherever it has a value, with the calibrated CAMS model filling gaps. Each hour is converted to AQI the same way.

CAMS calibration. CAMS is a coarse global model and often misreads the valley. Its PM2.5 is scaled by how it compared with your chosen station over the last 24 hours.

Inversion and trapped air. An inversion is a lid of warm air that traps cold air and pollution in the valley. Utah measures how well the valley can flush that out with the Clearing Index: mixing depth in hundreds of feet, multiplied by the wind through that layer in knots. NWS and Utah DAQ treat under 500 as poor ventilation, 500 to 1,000 as moderate and 1,000 or more as excellent. Open burning is restricted below 500 under Utah rule R307-202, which also uses 250 as its lowest winter cut-off.

Two separate signals. The AQI says what is in the air now. The Clearing Index says how fast new pollution can build. After a storm the air is usually clean even when high pressure has already dropped the Clearing Index, and it typically takes a few days of traffic and heating for pollution to build back up. So a low index over clean air only gets a quiet note. The Inversion tile reads not yet while the air is clean, building when the index is under 500 and the air is still good but more than 3 AQI points above its 24-hour average, active once the air is moderate or worse, lifting when the index is 500 or more and the air is unhealthy or improving from moderate, and none otherwise.

Official versus estimated. The Inversion tile and the solid steps on the Inversion chart are the official NWS daily values. The hourly strip and the dashed line are this page's own estimate from model mixing height and wind about 80 m up. That wind is weaker than the NWS transport wind, so each day is scaled to match the official afternoon value: the model gives the shape through the day, NWS sets the level.

Best time outside. The two-hour window with the lowest forecast PM2.5 that starts between 6 AM and 8 PM.

Smoke and ozone levels. Smoke is Light from 2 µg/m³, Moderate from 10 and Heavy from 35. Ozone is Moderate from 55 ppb, High from 71 and Very high from 86.

Updates. Data refreshes once an hour. If a source fails, the last good copy is kept while it still covers the hours ahead, and the failure is listed under Status.

A caution. Models tend to under-predict deep inversions. Trust Utah DAQ for whether it's a bad day, and the hourly lines for when.
